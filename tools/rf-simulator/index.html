What this cannot test A desk review with tapped labels does not test what an operator does with a scanner in one hand and a pallet in the other. operator-review.md asks for real bins with printed barcodes, and it is right. Use this bench for the sequence and the wording — how many scans a job costs, whether the short-pick form asks for the number they expect, what they do at an empty bin. Facilitator prompts

45 minutes

Say this first, and mean it: “This is a first version and we expect it to be wrong. You are not being tested — it is.” Then hand it over and stop talking. Every explanation you give is a finding you have destroyed.

1 · Cold start

Give it to them on the sign-in screen and say nothing.

  • Do they scan the device label unprompted?
  • Do they know what to do once signed in?

2 · Three jobs in a row

The interesting behaviour is the third job, not the first.

  • Do they read the instruction line, or go straight to the job details?
  • Do they scan what was asked, or the first label they see?

3 · The short pick — WT-1003

Twelve asked for, four on the shelf, no warning.

  • How long to find Report short?
  • Do they type what they found, or what is missing?
  • Write down the words they use for the reason, and use those words as the captions.

4 · The empty bin — WT-1005

  • Short with zero, hand back, or something else?
  • Ask which is right. Short-with-zero closes the job; handing back sends the next person to the same empty bin.

5 · Interruption

While they hold a job, tell them the supervisor needs them.

  • Hand back, or walk away holding it? Ask what they expect to happen either way.

6 · Afterwards

  • “What did this stop you doing that you can do today?”
  • “What did it make you do twice?”
  • “Which scan felt pointless — and which would you keep even if it were optional?”
